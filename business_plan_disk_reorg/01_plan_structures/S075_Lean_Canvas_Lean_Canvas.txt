What is a Lean Canvas?
Lean Canvas is a 1-page business model template created by Ash Maurya in 2010. It adapts Alexander Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas for startups operating under conditions of extreme uncertainty.
While the original Business Model Canvas maps an existing business holistically, Lean Canvas focuses on what matters most at the early stage: identifying and testing your riskiest assumptions before you build .
The 12 blocks of a Lean Canvas
Customer Segments — Who has the problem and who will pay
Problem — The top 3 problems your customers face
Existing Alternatives — How customers solve these problems today
Unique Value Proposition — The single, clear message that turns a visitor into a customer
Solution — Your top 3 features that address the top problems
Key Metrics — The numbers that tell you how your business is doing
Channels — How you reach your customers
Early Adopters — The specific subset of customers you target first
Cost Structure — Your fixed and variable costs
Revenue Streams — How you make money
Unfair Advantage — What can't be easily copied or bought
High-Level Concept — The X for Y analogy that makes your idea instantly understandable
Why Lean Canvas instead of Business Model Canvas?
Lean Canvas replaces four boxes from the original BMC — Key Partners, Key Activities, Key Resources, and Customer Relationships — with startup-specific ones: Problem, Solution, Key Metrics, and Unfair Advantage.
These substitutions matter. Early-stage ventures need to understand their problem deeply before worrying about key partners. They need to define metrics before cataloging resources. Lean Canvas captures what's most uncertain — and most risky.

Who uses Lean Canvas?
Over a million entrepreneurs, accelerators, and universities worldwide use Lean Canvas. It's a core component of the Running Lean methodology. Organizations like MassChallenge, Techstars, and hundreds of university programs use it to coach founders through early-stage validation.
